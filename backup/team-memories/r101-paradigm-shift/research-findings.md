## 1. Decision Heuristics -- When Do Great Hosts Offer vs Hold Back?

### The Comp Decision Algorithm (8 steps, synthesized from industry research)

1. **Establish baseline theo** -- Average Daily Theoretical (ADT) = avg_bet x hands_per_hour x house_edge. This is the STARTING point, not the answer.
2. **Assess current session** -- Game type, bet size, actual results (win/loss), session length, emotional state.
3. **Evaluate relationship history** -- First-time vs regular vs dormant. Did previous comps drive repeat visits?
4. **Read behavioral signals** -- Emotional state, loss-chasing signs, financial comfort, satisfaction level.
5. **Determine strategic purpose** -- Retention? Recruitment? Reactivation? Repair? Maintenance?
6. **Select comp tier + category** -- Standard, escalate, or hold back? Room, dining, entertainment, transport, or play credits?
7. **Execute with framing** -- Present as genuine recognition, not transactional exchange.
8. **Document for future** -- Update relationship notes for next interaction.

### When to OFFER (triggers)

| Signal | Comp Response | Why |
|--------|--------------|-----|
| Loyal regular has unusually bad session | Comp at HISTORICAL rate, not current theo | Preserves 3+ year relationship equity |
| First-time high-potential player | Moderate comp + "let me set up a relationship" | Recruitment without overcommitting |
| Player losing steadily for 3+ hours | Dining credit + "take a break, come back refreshed" | Reframes loss as recognition; extends on-property time |
| Dormant high-value player returns | Full package: room + dining + transport | Eliminates all friction to reignite relationship |
| Player expresses interest in competitor | Premium suite offer at own property | Defensive retention |
| Player celebrating (birthday, anniversary) | Upgrade + personalized touch | Emotional anchoring to property |

### When to HOLD BACK (triggers)

| Signal | Host Action | Why |
|--------|------------|-----|
| Player just won big | Let them self-fund experiences | Comp feels awkward when guest is flush |
| Loss-chasing behavior (increasing bets after losses) | Suggest break, NO additional play comps | Ethically inappropriate to incentivize distress |
| Erratic betting (swings from $100 to $5K) | Minimal comps pending more data | Unstable theo = unreliable future value |
| Unresolved service grievance | Address grievance FIRST, then comp | Comp during tension reads as dismissal |
| Player visibly distressed/fatigued | Non-gambling suggestion (dinner, show, rest) | Relationship preservation over theo extraction |
| Insufficient rating history (<3 days) | Conservative offer + future promise | 60-80% of player databases have <3 rated days |

### Comp Tier Framework

Standard allocation: 30-40% of theoretical loss OR 10% of actual loss, whichever is HIGHER.

| Player ADT | Room | Dining | Entertainment | Transport | Play Credits |
|-----------|------|--------|---------------|-----------|-------------|
| $100-200 | No (unless recruitment) | $50-75/visit | Standard shows | No | $10-25 free play |
| $200-500 | Standard room | $75-150/visit | Premium shows | No | $25-50 free play |
| $500-1000 | Suite upgrade | $150-300/visit | Premium + reserved | Airport transfer | $50-100 match play |
| $1000-5000 | Comp premium suite | $300-500/visit | VIP reserved + events | Round-trip limo | $100-250 |
| $5000+ | Multi-night suite package | $500+/week | Exclusive access | Full transport package | Negotiated |

### Critical Insight: Theo vs Actual Loss Divergence

When actual loss >> theo-predicted loss, hosts face a calibration moment. Pure theo says $500 in comps for a guest who lost $25,000 in 2 hours. That feels dismissively inadequate. Great hosts escalate to director approval for relationship-preserving comps (~$2,000-2,500 value using soft comps like rooms and dining that cost the casino 30-40% of face value).

**Key rule**: Comps are calculated on EXPECTED mathematical outcomes, not actual results. A player who loses $10K in 30 minutes gets FEWER comps than one who loses $5K over 6 hours of steady play. The steady player generates more theo.

### Game Type Matters

- **Slots**: 4-10% house edge. Higher theo per dollar wagered. Comp less aggressively (theo already high).
- **Blackjack**: 0.5-1.5% house edge. Lower theo but higher LTV players. Comp more aggressively to retain (competitor risk).
- **Baccarat**: 1.06% house edge. Fast pace, minimal skill. Most favorable comp ratio for casino.
- **Table game players** require more hosting attention to retain; slot players are behaviorally stickier.

## 2. Personal vs Scripted -- What Language Patterns Matter?

### The 7 Markers of Genuine Host Communication

**1. Anticipatory Service (proactive, not reactive)**
- SCRIPTED: "Is there anything I can help you with?"
- GENUINE: "I noticed you've been at the table for a while -- I reserved a table at [restaurant] for 8pm in case you'd like to take a break. Your favorite booth."
- KEY: References SPECIFIC knowledge (favorite booth, preferred restaurant, time patterns).

**2. Memory Continuity (references past interactions)**
- SCRIPTED: "Welcome back, valued guest!"
- GENUINE: "Sarah! How was your daughter's graduation? You mentioned it last time you were here in May."
- KEY: References PERSONAL details from previous conversations, not just play history.

**3. Emotional Mirroring (matches guest's state)**
- SCRIPTED: "Sorry to hear about your losses today."
- GENUINE: "That's a tough run. I've seen you play -- you make smart decisions. Sometimes the cards just don't cooperate. Let's get you set up at [restaurant] tonight, on me."
- KEY: Validates the person (smart decisions), externalizes the bad outcome (cards), offers concrete action.

**4. Comp Framing (recognition, not consolation)**
- SCRIPTED: "Based on your play, you qualify for a dining credit."
- GENUINE: "I want to make sure you know we appreciate you being here. I've set aside show tickets for Saturday -- I think you'd really enjoy it."
- KEY: Never mentions qualifying, earning, or play metrics. Frames as relationship, not transaction.

**5. Honest Boundary-Setting (transparent about constraints)**
- SCRIPTED: "I'm sorry, I can't do that."
- GENUINE: "That comp level is above what I can approve on my own -- but let me talk to my director. I want to make this work for you."
- KEY: Shows effort and advocacy rather than hiding behind policy.

**6. Name + Context Usage**
- SCRIPTED: Uses name once at greeting only.
- GENUINE: Weaves name naturally 2-3 times. "Mike, the chef at Seaside is doing a special tasting menu this weekend -- thought of you immediately."
- KEY: Name paired with REASON for thinking of them (not generic).

**7. Non-Gambling Engagement**
- SCRIPTED: Only contacts about promotions, play invitations.
- GENUINE: "Happy birthday! And congratulations on your son's wedding -- saw the photos. Next time you're here, first round is on me."
- KEY: Tracks life events. Relationship extends beyond the casino floor.

### What Kills Authenticity (Anti-Patterns)

| Pattern | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| "As a valued guest..." | Generic filler. Every email says this. | Reference something SPECIFIC about them. |
| Comp-first opening | Transactional. Guest feels like a revenue unit. | Lead with personal connection, comp comes naturally. |
| Template birthday messages | Obvious mass-send. | Add one personal detail. |
| Over-enthusiasm ("AMAZING!", "SO excited!") | Reads as fake. Hospitality, not infomercial. | Warm but measured. Confidence, not exclamation marks. |
| Ignoring losses, only celebrating wins | Guest feels unseen during hard moments. | Acknowledge difficulty with empathy, then reframe. |
| Mentioning play metrics to guest | Breaks the illusion. Guest doesn't want to feel tracked. | Never say "your ADT," "your theo," "based on your play level." |
| Same response regardless of context | One-size-fits-all signals no one is actually reading. | Tailor response to the SPECIFIC situation. |

### The Trust-Commitment Model

Research shows genuinely loyal casino customers have significantly higher TRUST and EMOTIONAL COMMITMENT than "spuriously loyal" patrons who return due to convenience or inertia. The difference:

- **True loyalty**: "My host KNOWS me. I wouldn't go anywhere else."
- **Spurious loyalty**: "I go there because it's close and I have points."

True loyalty is built through consistent personalization over time, not through comp volume. A $200 comp with genuine personal touch beats a $500 comp with generic delivery.

### Voice Register: What Top Hosts Sound Like

Based on research, the best hosts use a register that is:
- **Warm but not effusive** -- "Great to see you" not "OH MY GOD SO HAPPY YOU'RE HERE!"
- **Knowledgeable but not lecturing** -- Offers info as insider tips, not instructions
- **Confident but not presumptuous** -- "I think you'd enjoy..." not "You should definitely..."
- **Direct but not transactional** -- Gets to the point without corp-speak
- **Remembering but not surveilling** -- References past details naturally, never creepily

Angela Caprio (VP Casino Marketing, Resorts Atlantic City): "Making players feel important and comfortable at the property through honest conversations and genuine recognition."

4. **Accessibility** -- Being unable to reach a host when you need one is a top frustration.
5. **Recognition of loyalty over time** -- Long-term players want to be treated better than first-timers, not the same.

## 4. Key Insights -- Actionable Rules for Building a Casino Host AI

### Rule 1: Lead with Relationship, Comp Follows

Never open with the comp offer. Open with personal recognition, then transition to the comp as a natural expression of appreciation. The comp is the EVIDENCE of the relationship, not the relationship itself.

**For AI**: The agent should reference known guest details (name, occasion, preferences, past visits) BEFORE mentioning any comp or offer. Every turn should have at least one personal element.

### Rule 2: Match Comp Framing to Emotional State

- **After a loss**: Frame as "recognition of loyalty" not "consolation prize"
- **After a win**: Hold back or offer experience-based (show, dinner) not play-based
- **Celebrating**: Frame as "we want to be part of your celebration"
- **Frustrated**: Address frustration FIRST, then offer as "fresh start"
- **First visit**: Frame as "welcome, let me show you what we can do"

**For AI**: The agent needs sentiment-aware comp framing templates, not one-size-fits-all. The SAME comp ($50 dining credit) should be presented 5 different ways depending on context.

### Rule 3: Acknowledge the Pain, Don't Ignore It

When a guest has lost money, the worst response is to ignore it and pitch something else. The best response acknowledges ("tough run"), validates ("you play smart"), externalizes ("sometimes the cards don't cooperate"), and then offers concrete value.

**For AI**: The agent should NEVER skip acknowledgment of negative experiences. "I'm sorry to hear that" is insufficient. Must include validation + externalization + action.

### Rule 4: Never Sound Like a System

Guests can smell automation. The markers: "As a valued guest," "Based on your play level," "You qualify for," "Per our records." These phrases signal that a computer generated this, not a person who cares.

**For AI**: Blacklist corporate-speak phrases. Replace with conversational equivalents:
- "You qualify for" -> "I'd love to set you up with"
- "Based on your play" -> "I want to make sure you know how much we appreciate you being here"
- "As a valued guest" -> [Use their name and a specific detail instead]
- "Per our policy" -> "Let me see what I can do" / "Let me talk to my manager"

### Rule 5: Know When to Hold Back

A great host does NOT comp everything. They hold back when:
- Guest just won (let them enjoy it)
- Guest is chasing losses (ethical responsibility)
- Grievance is unresolved (address issue first)
- Relationship hasn't earned it yet (build incrementally)

**For AI**: The agent needs hold-back logic, not just offer logic. Comp strategy should include WHEN NOT TO OFFER, with specific sentiment/behavioral triggers.

### Rule 6: Anticipate, Don't React

Top hosts don't wait to be asked. They notice patterns and act preemptively:
- "You've been playing for 3 hours -- I reserved your favorite table at [restaurant]"
- "I saw the weather forecast for your trip -- I moved your tee time to avoid the rain"
- "Your anniversary is next month -- want me to plan something special?"

**For AI**: The agent should generate proactive suggestions based on conversation context, time-of-day, known preferences, and occasion data -- without being asked. This is the single biggest differentiator between average and great hosting.

### Rule 7: Comp the Relationship, Not the Transaction

The comp allocation should reflect the CUMULATIVE relationship, not just today's play. A 3-year loyal regular who has a bad night deserves comp at their historical rate, not today's depressed theo.

**For AI**: The agent should weight relationship tenure and consistency when framing offers, not just current-turn context. Multi-turn profiling data becomes the comp calibration input.
